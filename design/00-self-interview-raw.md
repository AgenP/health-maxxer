# Self-interview raw notes (2026-10-11)

Method: Teresa Torres story-based interviewing. Interviewee = interviewer's user = Philip (self-only constraint; highest bias risk).

## Q1: last time you thought about plastic/harm in food or drink
> "right now. Travelling in the U.S. I asked for bottled water at my hotel and got given one with plastic in it. At the hackathon I had bottled water from a plastic bottle. I just found a metal cup, and took water from a machine."
> "I almost always opt for tap water in Scotland ... but in the U.S. I'm not sure of the quality of the tap water, and even in Scotland I may be overconfident and ignoring case-by-case / city-to-city differences and latest news which could keep me better informed"

Stories surfaced: (1) hotel bottled water, (2) hackathon plastic bottle then metal cup + water machine, (3) tap water habit in Scotland.

## Parking lot (solution ideas, not needs)
- "Machine" input: photo of the water machine / dispenser, analyse its spec sheet for a microplastic estimate; ideally a direct lab result.

## Q2: the hackathon moment, bottle to metal cup
> "I wanted more water, couldnt find more bottled water and saw someone at a water machine getting water then looked for something I could use to hold the water (instead of the plastic bottle I just used) and found a WeWork metal cup"

Observations: trigger was running out (bottles unavailable), not concern; social cue (someone else at the machine) revealed the option; searched for a vessel "instead of the plastic bottle I just used".

Correction (Q3 attempt): interviewer assumed Philip was holding the empty bottle; he was not. Leading premise withdrawn.

## Q3: what were you looking for / what next
> "looking for a cup. Once done I came back to my computer which is where I am now"

Observation: low-deliberation moment; once a non-plastic option existed, the switch was effortless and the question was closed (no further thought about the machine water itself, as far as reported).

## Q4: the hotel
> "2 water bottles from the front desk. Now that I reflect on this, I probably could have go to the bar which was open and asked for a cup, but I wanted one for the next day (today)"

Observations: the need was portable water for tomorrow; plastic bottles won on portability and availability. A non-plastic route (bar + cup) existed but didn't solve "carry it tomorrow". "Now that I reflect" = hindsight; in the moment the plastic question was not salient.

## Q5: a specific tap decision on this trip
> "in my hotel room. Thats when I decided to ask for bottled water. Enough for current thirst and for the morning before I get outside and can somewhere where I can buy a drink"

Observations: the hotel-room tap is the real origin of the hotel story. Uncertainty about local tap water pushed him to plastic bottled water, swapping an unknown risk (tap) for a known-but-unweighed one (plastic). Quantity planned to bridge until he could buy a drink outside.

## Q6: tap to front desk
> "I went downstairs directly to the front desk"

Observation: no lookup, no research step; the decision was immediate.
