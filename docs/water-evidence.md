# Water evidence: tap vs bottled vs water machine (NYC hotel)

Format: claim | figure | source | strength. Gathered 2026-10-11.

## 1. Micro/nanoplastics: bottled vs tap
- Bottled water nanoplastics (Qian et al. 2024, PNAS; 3 unnamed US brands, 5 samples each, SRS microscopy) | "110,000 to 370,000 [press: 400,000] particles/L, avg ~240,000; ~90% nanoplastics" | https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2300582121 | peer-reviewed; figures from press coverage (https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/scientists-find-about-a-quarter-million-invisible-microplastic-particles-in-a-liter-of-bottled-water), paper itself 403'd: UNVERIFIED against primary. No tap comparison in this study.
- Bottled vs tap, same lab (Mason et al. 2018, Front. Chem.; 259 bottles, 11 brands, 9 countries) | ">100 um: 10.4 particles/L bottled vs 5.45 tap (~2x)"; incl. 6.5-100 um: ~325/L bottled; 93% of bottles contaminated | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6141690/ | peer-reviewed (not journal-reviewed when first released by Orb Media; small fraction Nile Red only, not spectroscopically confirmed)
- Annual intake (Cox et al. 2019, ES&T, "Human Consumption of Microplastics") | "bottled-water-only drinkers may ingest an additional 90,000 particles/yr vs 4,000 for tap-only" | https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.9b01517 | peer-reviewed; figure from ACS summary, paper not opened: UNVERIFIED against primary
- Caveat: methods differ by orders of magnitude in size cutoff (Mason >6.5 um; Qian down to ~nm). Do not compare 240k/L to 5.45/L directly. Health impact unknown.

## 2. NYC tap (DEP 2025 Drinking Water Supply and Quality Report)
URL: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dep/downloads/pdf/water/drinking-water/drinking-water-supply-quality-report/2025-drinking-water-supply-quality-report.pdf | official (read directly)
- "There is no lead in New York City's water distribution system"; lead comes from building service lines/plumbing.
- Residential lead tap sampling: 90th percentile 10 ug/L (AL 15), range ND-155, 16 of 326 sites exceeded AL; no system exceedance.
- Advice: "RUN YOUR TAP for 30 seconds to 2 minutes ... when your water has been sitting for several hours"; "USE COLD WATER ... Hot tap water is more likely to contain lead."
- PFOA: ND-2.7 ng/L, single detection (1/8/2025, Van Cortlandt Village), not detected in quarterly UCMR5 sampling. EPA 2024 MCL = 4.0 ng/L for PFOA/PFOS, so below it. PFOS: reported ND.

## 3. EWG Tap Water Database, NYC
- "22 contaminants detected, 8 above EWG health guidelines; 7 of the 8 are disinfection byproducts (TTHMs, HAAs, chloroform etc.)" | https://www.ewg.org/tapwater/8324-article-whats-new-york-citys-drinking-water (system page https://www.ewg.org/tapwater/system.php?pws=NY7003493) | advocacy; 403 on fetch, figures from search snippet: UNVERIFIED. EWG guidelines are far stricter than legal limits; NYC meets all federal/state MCLs.

## 4. Water dispensers/coolers
- Isfahan bottled-water coolers | mean HPC 38,864 CFU/mL; 62% exceeded acceptable level; coolers significantly higher than tap (P<0.05) | https://ijph.tums.ac.ir/index.php/ijph/article/download/4082/3741 | peer-reviewed (small, regional)
- Budapest healthcare dispensers (36) | moulds/yeasts in 86.8% of samples; HPC elevated vs mains | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8308914/ | peer-reviewed
- Takeaway: poorly maintained/idle dispensers grow bacteria; no good microplastic data for filtered point-of-use coolers found: UNVERIFIED.

## 5. Heat and PET leaching
- Antimony: no migration at 4/20 C; significant at 40 C (under EU 5 ug/L); at 60 C after 30 days EU limit exceeded | https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Migration%20of%20antimony%20from%20polyethylene%20terephthalate%20used%20in%20mineral%20water%20bottles.pdf | peer-reviewed (via secondary copy)
- 3 PET brands, 25-60 C, 200 days: 0.02-2.14 ug/L Sb; max at 60 C exceeded Japan's 2.0 ug/L | https://watersa.net/article/view/22067 | peer-reviewed
- Heat raising microplastic release from PET: not confirmed this pass: UNVERIFIED.

## Recommendation basis
NYC tap (cold, run 30s-2min if sitting) is officially compliant and has ~half the >100 um microplastics of bottled in the only same-method comparison; bottled adds plastic particle load and heat-driven antimony; dispensers are fine only if maintained.
