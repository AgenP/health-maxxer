- Away: defaulted to plastic bottled water within minutes, with no lookup.

## Opportunities (needs, in his voice; all assumptions)
- **O1.** "When I'm somewhere new, I don't know if the tap water is OK, so I default to plastic bottles." *(hotel)*
- **O2.** "I want to know that the trade I'm making (tap vs bottled vs machine) is actually the better one." *(hotel, hackathon)*
- **O3.** "I want water I can carry for later without it being plastic." *(hotel: bottles were for "the next day")*
- **O4.** "At home I might be overconfident: city-to-city differences and recent news could change the answer." *(Scotland, Q1)*
- **O5.** "I'm not sure my past research (my bottle) holds up." *(Scotland)*

## Insights
- **The stories are about water and location, not packaged food.** Every story is water, and the decisive variable each time was *where he was*.
- **Uncertainty pushes him to plastic.** An unknown risk (the tap) loses to a known but unweighed one (the plastic bottle). Nothing showed him the comparison.
- **The decision takes seconds and involves no lookup.** Any tool has to answer at the tap or desk, in one glance.
- **When a non-plastic option is visible and easy, he takes it** (the hackathon cup). Visibility beats persuasion.
- **His home habit already matches lab-tested behaviours**: running the tap first and using a chosen vessel. PlasticList tests both, comparing 0 s vs 30 s of running and vessels such as Hydro Flask, Nalgene and Solo cup.

## Parking lot (solution ideas raised in the interview)
